# Distractor corpus test, protocol (draft 1, 29 October 2026)

Status: written before any run. Nothing here has been measured. Change it only by adding a dated note at the bottom, never by editing a rule after seeing results.

Working title: Your RAG Pipeline Worked Until the Corpus Grew

## 1. Question

Hold the retrieval method fixed. Add more documents that do not answer the question. How do evidence retrieval and answer accuracy change, and does it matter whether the added documents look like the answer?

Two parts of the answer to find out. Part one is how much the result falls as the corpus grows. Part two is whether look-alike distractors (close in topic, but not answering) hurt more than ordinary ones, and whether parent document retrieval reduces the damage.

## 2. Data and the question set

Reuse the frozen set from `agentic-rag-eval` (hashes in `data_full/FROZEN.txt`).

- 2,000 recent arXiv papers (categories cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.IR; July to September 2026). The document is the abstract.
- 298 questions, each written from one paper, hand checked, with q064 and q088 removed. Three styles: specific, problem, vague.
- The question list is locked now. No question is added, dropped or reworded after any result in this study is seen. The pilot uses the first 10 questions in qid order, chosen by this rule and by nothing else.

Possible ceiling to check. In the earlier test, plain dense search over whole abstracts put the right paper in its top 5 for 95.6 percent of the 298 questions on the full 2,000 papers. For that one setup, recall cannot fall by more than about 4.4 points between a tiny corpus and the full one, because recall cannot exceed 100 percent. The new design adds BM25 and chunked retrieval, whose recall on 2,000 papers is not known, so this cap is not assumed for the other cells. The free pilot shows how much room each cell has. See section 9.

## 3. Corpus construction

For every question, the corpus is the gold paper plus N minus 1 distractors. Sizes N: 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 1,801 (and 2,000 for look-alike only), and larger sizes if the pool is extended (section 9). Corpora are nested, so a larger corpus contains the smaller one.

Two conditions per question:

- **Look-alike.** Take the non-gold papers in order of similarity to the gold abstract and keep the first N minus 1. No randomness.
- **Ordinary.** Draw N minus 1 papers at random from the non-gold papers ranked 200 or lower in similarity to the gold abstract. Three draws with fixed seeds 1, 2 and 3. Same draws are reused for every method and representation, so every comparison is paired.

Ordinary distractors come from papers ranked 200 or lower, which leaves 1,800 papers, so the largest ordinary corpus is N = 1,801. Both conditions therefore run at 100, 250, 500, 1,000 and 1,801. At 1,801 the look-alike corpus includes the 199 nearest papers and the ordinary one does not. N = 2,000 is the whole collection and runs for look-alike only, as an anchor, not a comparison. Look-alike is deterministic and runs once. Ordinary runs with seeds 1, 2 and 3.

**Similarity for selection must be independent of what is tested.** Use a frozen embedding model that is not the tested embedding model and not a lexical score, so the corpus is not built to fool one system. Name, version and settings go in the repository. I will also report one sensitivity run with the selection model swapped, to show the finding does not hinge on this choice.

**What look-alike means.** Look-alike here means close to the gold abstract. This does not depend on how the question is worded, and it is reproducible. It tests competition from papers that resemble the answer paper, not necessarily papers that resemble the question, and the article names this limit. A second run ranks by similarity to the question, using the same independent model, and both are reported.

**Name.** Call these look-alike distractors, not near-duplicates. They are close in topic, and must not answer the question.

## 4. Checking that distractors do not answer the question

Support means: the abstract alone would let a reader answer the question.

1. Automatic screen. A language model reads the question and each of the 50 most similar non-gold abstracts and answers yes, no or unsure for support. Any yes or unsure goes to step 2.
2. Review. I review every yes and unsure, plus a random sample of the no answers. The sample size and the number of disagreements are reported.
3. Action. A distractor found to support the answer is replaced, not just removed. It is filtered out of that question's ranked candidate list before any corpus is built, so each corpus is the gold paper plus the first N minus 1 non-supporting papers in order. N stays exact and corpora stay nested. A paper that supports the answer as well as the gold paper is excluded from the pool, not added as a second correct paper, so recall stays defined on the one gold paper. Report how many papers were replaced per question.
4. Beyond the top 50, the screen relies on the sample audit only. This is a stated limit. Hidden support is most likely among the closest papers, which the screen covers fully.
5. Reuse the earlier column `other_papers_that_also_answer` in `questions_review.csv` as the starting list.

## 5. Methods and representations

Two retrieval methods times two representations, four cells:

| | Flat chunks | Parent document |
|---|---|---|
| BM25 | A | B |
| Embeddings (`BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`) | C | D |

- A chunk is a window of 2 sentences with 1 sentence of overlap. The parent is the full abstract.
- Flat returns the top 5 chunks, then maps each to its paper for scoring.
- Parent retrieves chunks, maps them to papers, removes repeats, and returns the top 5 distinct papers.
- This gives flat fewer distinct papers when two top chunks come from one paper. I report it as part of what flat chunks means. A flat variant that collects chunks until it has 5 distinct papers returns the same ranked papers as parent, by construction, so no separate cell is run. The two differ only in the text given to a generator. Flat rows record how many distinct papers its 5 chunk slots covered.
- For answer generation, the same context token budget (to be set from the pilot) is given to every cell. Flat fills it with chunks, parent with whole abstracts.
- Corpus sizes, distractor draws, questions and top k stay fixed within a comparison.

## 6. Metrics

Retrieval, free to compute (local models only):

- **Evidence Recall@5.** Share of questions with at least one supporting paper in the top 5.
- **First supporting rank**, reported as the mean reciprocal rank and as the share ranked first.
- Index size and search time per query, since the corpus grows.

Answer step, paid, run after the retrieval results are in:

- **Answer correctness**, judged against the gold abstract by one fixed judge, separate from recall. This tells us whether retrieved evidence was used correctly. Run on the cells and sizes chosen after retrieval results, with the cost agreed first.

## 7. Uncertainty

- The main interval comes from resampling the 298 questions with replacement (paired bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, fixed seed), applied to differences between sizes, conditions and cells.
- Report each of the three ordinary draws separately. The question bootstrap does not capture all uncertainty from which distractors were chosen, and the article says so.
- Three draws alone are not treated as an interval.

## 8. Breaking point, fixed in advance

This is a threshold set in advance, not a limit of the system discovered by the test. Describe it as the smallest tested size that meets the rule, never as where the pipeline breaks. For a cell it is the smallest corpus size at which both hold, compared with the smallest size N = 100:

1. Evidence Recall@5 is at least 5 points lower.
2. The paired bootstrap 95 percent interval of the difference lies entirely below zero.

If no size meets both, the article says there was no breaking point in the range tested, and gives the largest drop with its interval. I will not describe a threshold in any other way.

## 9. Open decisions for the author

1. **Collection size.** Default: stay with the 2,000 papers for the pilot. Extend only if the pilot shows the range is too narrow (for plain dense search the room is at most about 4.4 points, and the other cells are not yet known). An extension would add about 10,000 recent papers from the same categories with `collect_papers.py` (free, but arXiv asks for a wait between requests) and embed them locally. Every new paper is a possible hidden answer, so the screen in section 4 matters more.
2. **Domain.** Default: keep arXiv AI papers, because the questions are already written and reviewed. The paper behind this idea is about biomedical text. Switching would need new questions and cost real money for writing and review.
3. **Answer step.** Default: retrieval for all cells and sizes (free), and answer correctness only for the most informative cells (paid, cost agreed first).

## 10. Run order and spend

1. Lock this protocol. Show it to the author.
2. Retrieval pilot, 10 questions, sizes 100 and 1,000, one draw, all four cells. Local only, no paid calls. This is the only free step.
3. Distractor screen on the pilot questions only. It is paid: 10 questions times 50 candidates is 500 checks (the full set is 298 times 50, which is 14,900). Print the call count and an estimate from list prices before any call, then ask for a spend cap before running it or any larger paid step.
4. Full retrieval run, then the screen, then the answer step if agreed.

## 11. What the article may claim

Only what the numbers show, with the ranges. The reader problem is a pipeline that does well on a small demonstration collection and worse after ingestion grows. A null result is a result: if recall barely moves, the article says that small collections hide little, and shows where the measured room ran out.
